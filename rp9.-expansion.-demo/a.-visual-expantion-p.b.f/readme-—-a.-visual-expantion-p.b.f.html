META-ABSTRACT

Abstract

This document forms part of A. META EXPANTION, within A. VISUAL EXPANTION P.B.F, and functions as the governing README for the complete four-document group: two visual presentations and two accompanying informational documents.

Its function is to define the shared scope, functional boundary, document roles, reading paths, visual language, source relation, and file architecture through which P.B.F. relations are visually represented and their conceptual, recursive, dimensional, and relational readings are explained.

The visual material operates from the already established P.B.F. functional basis. It does not establish another generative P.B.F. sequence and does not make geometry, visualization, scale, or representation the origin of the function being represented.

Within 2. RP9. EXPANSION, this folder occupies a visual and representational position under A. META EXPANTION. Its material may relate to functional, relational, structural, or computational material located elsewhere in the Expansion where those relations are relevant to the visual representation.

The governing distinction is:

ESTABLISHED P.B.F. FUNCTION↓VISUAL / GEOMETRIC REPRESENTATION

The representation may change while the established functional basis remains preserved. The four documents occupy complementary positions within one relational group; their numbering identifies their presentation positions rather than a mandatory derivational sequence.

1. STATUS AND BOUNDARY

A. VISUAL EXPANTION P.B.F is the visual and accompanying informational expansion component within A. META EXPANTION.

The folder groups documents by their shared relational subject. A.1 and A.2 make the relations visually inspectable. A.3 and A.4 explain the conceptual and representational distinctions needed to read those visual constructions.

Visual presentation and informational explanation are complementary functions within this group. The four documents remain at the same organizational level.

Its shared function is to make P.B.F. relations visually inspectable and informationally readable through different:

DIRECTIONS

DIMENSIONS

GEOMETRIES

FIELDS

SCALES

GROUPINGS

POSITIONS

RELATIONAL READINGS

The visual material does not establish a new primary function.

It does not replace the upstream P.B.F. derivation.

It does not replace computational execution or validation.

It does not make a later visual or geometric construction the origin of a relation already established elsewhere.

The governing boundary is:

P.B.F. ESTABLISHES THE FUNCTION↓VISUAL EXPANTION REPRESENTSTHE ESTABLISHED FUNCTION

Therefore:

VISUAL REPRESENTATION≠FUNCTIONAL ORIGIN

7. VISUAL LANGUAGE

Where a visual series uses shared colors, symbols, centers, arrows, lines, paths, or repeated structures, those elements function as representational identifiers.

They do not independently establish the relation being represented.

A shared visual language should therefore preserve:

  1. consistent positional identification;
  2. consistent distinction between equal and differentiated positions;
  3. clear directional reading;
  4. clear center or reference positions where applicable;
  5. separation between functional relation and geometric form;
  6. separation between exact relation and illustrative scale;
  7. continuity across related visual representations.

The governing rule is:

VISUAL IDENTIFIER≠FUNCTION IDENTIFIED

8. VISUAL CONSTRUCTION RULES

Each visual representation should:

  1. remain independently readable;
  2. identify its local visual function;
  3. preserve the established relation being represented;
  4. distinguish function from geometric representation;
  5. distinguish exact relation from illustrative representation where required;
  6. show only the relations required by that visual position;
  7. avoid introducing unsupported numerical or functional claims;
  8. remain legible at its intended presentation scale;
  9. preserve relevant source relations;
  10. remain consistent with the governing Expansion reading standard.

The default direction is:

ESTABLISHED FUNCTION / RELATION↓VISUAL EXPRESSION↓GEOMETRIC / REPRESENTATIONAL READING

The direction must not be reversed unless a specific local relation explicitly establishes otherwise.

10. FOUR-DOCUMENT GROUP AND READING ACCESS

The four documents provide distinct, complementary access to the same functional and relational architecture.

Document Primary presentation function Contribution to the group
A. 1. P.B.F. — VISUAL EXPANTION Geometric and proportional visual presentation Presents linear, bidirectional, planar, square, cubic, circle-square, scale, and Rx8 readings of the established relations.
A. 2. P.B.F. — IMAGES FRACTAL EXPANTION Recursive and fractal image sequence Develops the image series from functional orientation through the minimum recursive unit, next-scale continuation, four-direction projection, complete Square-Bit, and abstract fractal field.
A. 3. P.B.F — MATHEMATICS, FUNCTION, AND DIMENSIONAL REPRESENTATION Dimensional and representational explanation Explains the relation between generative function and representation, recursive units and spatial arrangements, structural counts and quotients, division and connection, and primary states and representational depth.
A. 4. P.B.F — POTENTIAL → PRIMARY BINARY FUNCTION Conceptual foundation and functional reading key Presents Potential, primary conceptual differentiation, binary representation, recursive expansion, structural transformation, and their accompanying visual readings.

These functions explain why the documents belong together. The informational documents carry reading distinctions used by the visual documents, while the visual documents make those relations inspectable through position, direction, scale, and geometry.

The document roles describe their main emphasis. A.1 and A.2 include explanatory text; A.3 and A.4 also include formulas, structural arrangements, and visual or schematic readings.

10.1. Visual reading path

For an initial visual overview, read:

README→A.1→A.2

A.1 provides the geometric and proportional overview. A.2 develops the recursive and fractal image sequence. Within A.2, follow its assigned image groups and accompanying support texts in order.

Use A.3 and A.4 when the corresponding conceptual or representational distinction requires clarification.

10.2. Conceptual reading path

For a foundation-first reading, read:

README→A.4→A.3

A.4 introduces the conceptual sequence. A.3 explicitly continues that architecture through dimensional and relational readings. Then consult A.1 and A.2 for the corresponding visual expressions.

10.3. Numbering and local dependencies

The labels A.1–A.4 identify the documents within the folder. They do not require every reader to follow a single four-step derivational sequence.

The continuation from A.4 to A.3 is a stated local reading relation. The ordered image groups within A.2 are another local sequence. Neither changes the shared organizational level of the four documents.

The governing distinction is:

FOLDER POSITION≠FUNCTIONAL DEPENDENCY ORDER

12. ABSOLUTE VISUAL LOCK

The complete Visual Expansion must preserve the following distinction:

ESTABLISHED P.B.F. FUNCTION↓MULTIPLE VISUAL REPRESENTATIONS↓ONE PRESERVED FUNCTIONAL BASIS

Therefore:

GEOMETRY≠FUNCTIONAL ORIGIN
VISUAL EXPANSION≠NEW PRIMARY FUNCTION
REPRESENTATIONAL VARIATION≠FUNCTIONAL REPLACEMENT

The visual field may expand.

The direction may change.

The dimensional representation may change.

The scale may change.

The grouping may change.

The geometric form may change.

The visual reading may change.

None of these changes, by themselves, establish another primary origin.
